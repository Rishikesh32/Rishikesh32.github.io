Data visualization

Data visualization is the interface between complex quantitative calculations and human decision-making. Picking the right chart, choosing perceptually accurate visual encodings, designing accessible color palettes, and annotating clear takeaways turns dense tables into compelling, trustworthy insights.

In this area

    Effective data visualization is grounded in perceptual psychology and graphical integrity. The diagram below illustrates how chart choice, perceptual hierarchy, color encoding, and direct annotation work together to communicate insights clearly.

    07.1 · Form follows function

    Choosing the right chart

    Selecting the wrong chart type distorts the message before the reader has even read a label. The business question you are answering dictates the visual structure you must use.

    Core Chart Families & Their Purpose

    • Comparison across categories: Horizontal bar chart or column chart sorted by value. Bars start at zero to maintain proportional fidelity.
    • Change over continuous time: Line chart with time on the horizontal axis. Emphasizes rate of change, trends, and turning points.
    • Distribution & spread: Histograms (for single variables), box plots, or violin plots (for comparing distributions across cohorts).
    • Correlation & relationship: Scatter plot with optional trend lines, highlighting clusters, outliers, and variance.
    • Part-to-whole share: Stacked 100% bar chart or waterfall chart. Avoid pie charts with more than 3 slices.

    The Multi-Slice Pie Chart Trap

    Pie charts with 8 or 10 slices force readers to compare subtle angular differences between curved wedges. Converting that same data into a descending horizontal bar chart lets the reader instantly rank items from top to bottom and judge exact differences in length effortlessly.

    07.2 · Visual psychology

    Perception & encoding

    Graphical perception is the visual decoding of quantitative information. Seminal research by William Cleveland and Robert McGill ranked visual encodings from most accurate to least accurate.

    The Visual Encoding Hierarchy

    1. Position along a common aligned scale (e.g. standard bar chart, scatter plot) — Highest accuracy
    2. Position along non-aligned scales (e.g. small multiples panels)
    3. Length / direction (e.g. gauge lines, slope charts)
    4. Angle / slope (e.g. pie chart wedges, radar charts)
    5. Area (e.g. bubble charts, treemaps) — Moderate error
    6. Volume / 3D depth (e.g. 3D rendered bars) — Severe perceptual distortion
    7. Color saturation & hue — Lowest accuracy for numbers; excellent for categories

    Preattentive Attributes

    Certain visual properties—such as color hue, stroke thickness, and spatial position—are processed by the human visual cortex in under 200 milliseconds before conscious focus occurs. Use a single pop of vibrant accent color against muted gray bars to guide the viewer's eye directly to the insight.

    07.3 · Communicating with color

    Color & accessibility

    Color is one of the most powerful communication tools in analytics, but also the most frequently abused. Using color with purpose requires understanding palette types and designing for accessibility.

    The Three Palette Archetypes

    • Categorical (Qualitative): Distinct hues with equal visual weight (e.g. blue, amber, teal) to distinguish unrelated groups like channels or operating systems.
    • Sequential: A single hue varying smoothly in lightness and saturation (e.g. light blue to dark blue) to encode ordered numerical values like population density or order frequency.
    • Diverging: Two contrasting hues meeting at a neutral, light midpoint (e.g. dark red to white/gray to dark teal) to display deviation from a baseline (such as net margin, budget variance, or growth rate).

    Color Vision Deficiency (CVD) Awareness

    Roughly 8% of men and 0.5% of women have red-green color blindness (deuteranomaly or protanomaly). Relying solely on pure red and pure green to indicate negative and positive ensures that 1 in 12 viewers cannot distinguish success from failure. Always pair color with secondary visual cues (icons like ▲ / ▼, direct text labels) or use accessible palettes like Viridis or ColorBrewer blue-orange.

    07.4 · Code-driven graphics

    Plotting in Python

    Python offers a rich ecosystem of visualization libraries. Modern analysts match the tool to the task: Matplotlib for granular layout control, Seaborn for statistical exploration, and Plotly for interactive web delivery.

    The Python Visualization Toolkit

    • Matplotlib: The foundation of scientific plotting. Uses an object-oriented canvas (Figure and Axes) that grants pixel-perfect control over spine visibility, tick formatting, custom annotations, and publication figures.
    • Seaborn: High-level statistical library built on Matplotlib. Integrates seamlessly with Pandas DataFrames to plot confidence intervals, categorical facet grids, and regression distributions with single function calls.
    • Plotly: Declarative interactive plotting library rendering via WebGL/D3. Powers responsive hover tooltips, zoom/pan exploration, and interactive filtering in web applications and notebooks.
    import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import seaborn as sns

# Clean, decluttered publication styling
fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(8, 4.5), dpi=150)
sns.lineplot(data=df, x='month', y='mrr', hue='tier', ax=ax, palette='viridis')

# Direct labeling and decluttering
ax.spines[['top', 'right']].set_visible(False)
ax.set_title("Enterprise MRR Grew 42% in H2", fontsize=12, fontweight='bold', loc='left')
plt.show()

    07.5 · Graphical integrity

    Misleading charts

    Misleading visualizations can be intentional manipulation or accidental carelessness. A professional analyst maintains strict graphical integrity to preserve stakeholder trust.

    Common Visualization Traps to Avoid

    • Truncated Bar Baselines: Cutting the y-axis on a bar chart distorts visual proportions. If Company A has $100M and Company B has $105M, starting the y-axis at $95M makes Company B look three times larger.
    • 3D Perspective Distortion: Tilting a pie or bar chart in 3D perspective foreshortens items in the background and magnifies slices in the foreground, corrupting human area perception.
    • Dual-Axis Manipulation: Pairing two unrelated metrics on separate left and right y-axes allows the author to arbitrarily scale and shift lines to imply causal correlation or false intersection points.
    • Area Scaling of 1D Quantities: Doubling the diameter of a circle quadruples its area (\(A = \pi r^2\)), visually exaggerating a 2x change into a 4x perception.

    07.6 · Framing and takeaway

    Annotating & telling the story

    A chart without context is a puzzle the reader has to solve. Visual storytelling guides the viewer's attention directly to the insight through decluttering, direct labeling, and active headlines.

    Principles of Visual Storytelling

    1. The Active Headline: Replace passive titles like "Revenue by Region Q1-Q4" with active takeaways: "West Region led annual growth (+24%) following store expansion".
    2. Direct Labeling: Eliminate disconnected legend boxes. Place category names and values directly at the end of lines or adjacent to bars to remove ocular gymnastics.
    3. Maximize the Data-Ink Ratio: Remove heavy border boxes, saturated backgrounds, zebra striping, and redundant gridlines so the data itself is the highest-contrast element.
    4. Call Out the Inflection Point: Use subtle callout lines or shaded background zones to explain why a line jumped or dipped (e.g. "Pricing change launched on March 15th").
