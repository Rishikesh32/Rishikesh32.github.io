Stochastic Calculus & Asset Dynamics

Brownian motion, Itô's Lemma, Girsanov theorem, and mean-reverting jump-diffusion processes.

In this area

    Stochastic calculus is the mathematical language of continuous-time asset pricing, derivatives, and algorithmic volatility trading. From Itô's Lemma and Girsanov's change of measure to jump-diffusion and Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes, it governs how quants price and hedge dynamic market risk.

    2.1

    Brownian Motion & Wiener Process

    Standard Brownian Motion (Wiener Process) $W_t$ is a continuous-time stochastic process with $W_0 = 0$, independent increments $W_t - W_s \sim \mathcal{N}(0, t-s)$, and continuous but nowhere differentiable sample paths. Its quadratic variation over $[0, t]$ is deterministic: $[W, W]_t = t$.

    Mathematical Formulation

    $W_t - W_s \sim \mathcal{N}(0, t-s), \quad (dW_t)^2 = dt, \quad dW_t \cdot dt = 0$
    • Nowhere differentiability means standard Newton-Leibniz calculus fails: ordinary chain rules do not apply.
    • Martingale property: $\mathbb{E}[W_t | \mathcal{F}_s] = W_s$ for $s \le t$.
    • Scaling invariance: $\frac{1}{\sqrt{c}} W_{ct}$ is also a standard Brownian motion.
    2.2

    Itô's Lemma & Stochastic Integration

    Itô's Lemma is the stochastic Taylor expansion chain rule. For a twice continuously differentiable function $f(t, X_t)$ where $dX_t = \mu_t dt + \sigma_t dW_t$, the non-zero quadratic variation $(dW_t)^2 = dt$ produces an essential second-order drift term $\frac{1}{2} \sigma_t^2 \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2} dt$.

    Mathematical Formulation

    $df(t, X_t) = \left( \frac{\partial f}{\partial t} + \mu_t \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} + \frac{1}{2} \sigma_t^2 \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2} \right) dt + \sigma_t \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} dW_t$
    • The convexity term $\frac{1}{2} \sigma^2 f''(x)$ represents Gamma in option pricing and explains option time decay (Theta).
    • Applied to $f(S) = \ln S$ where $dS = \mu S dt + \sigma S dW$, Itô's Lemma yields $d(\ln S) = (\mu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2)dt + \sigma dW$.
    • Multi-dimensional Itô Lemma accounts for cross-asset correlations $\rho_{ij} dt = dW_i dW_j$.
    2.3

    Girsanov's Theorem & Risk-Neutral Measures

    Girsanov's Theorem allows transforming probability measures, shifting the drift of a stochastic process without changing its volatility or impossible events (null sets). In finance, it changes the historical real-world measure $\mathbb{P}$ with drift $\mu$ into the risk-neutral measure $\mathbb{Q}$ where all discounted assets drift at the risk-free rate $r$.

    Mathematical Formulation

    $\left. \frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\mathbb{P}} \right|_{\mathcal{F}_t} = \exp\left( -\int_0^t \lambda_s dW_s - \frac{1}{2} \int_0^t \lambda_s^2 ds \right), \quad \lambda = \frac{\mu - r}{\sigma}$
    • $\lambda = \frac{\mu - r}{\sigma}$ is the market price of risk (Sharpe ratio of the underlying asset).
    • Under $\mathbb{Q}$, the discounted price process $e^{-rt} S_t$ is a true martingale, enabling expectation pricing $\mathbb{E}^\mathbb{Q}[e^{-rT} Payoff]$.
    • Fundamental Theorem of Asset Pricing (FTAP): No arbitrage is equivalent to the existence of at least one equivalent martingale measure $\mathbb{Q}$.
    2.4

    Geometric Brownian Motion (GBM)

    Geometric Brownian Motion (GBM) models asset prices such that returns are log-normally distributed, preventing prices from going negative: $dS_t = \mu S_t dt + \sigma S_t dW_t$. The analytical solution is $S_t = S_0 \exp\left((\mu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2)t + \sigma W_t\right)$.

    Mathematical Formulation

    $S_t = S_0 \exp\left( \left(\mu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2\right)t + \sigma W_t \right)$
    • Expected value $\mathbb{E}[S_t] = S_0 e^{\mu t}$, while median value is $S_0 e^{(\mu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2)t}$.
    • Limitation: Real financial markets exhibit fat tails (leptokurtosis) and volatility clustering, which pure GBM underestimates.
    • Forms the underlying asset model for the Black-Scholes-Merton option pricing framework.
    2.5

    Ornstein-Uhlenbeck Mean-Reverting Process

    The Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) process models mean-reverting time series, fundamental to statistical arbitrage pairs trading, interest rates (Vasicek model), and volatility modeling: $dX_t = \theta(\mu - X_t)dt + \sigma dW_t$, where $\theta$ is the speed of mean reversion, $\mu$ is the long-term equilibrium, and $\sigma$ is diffusion volatility.

    Mathematical Formulation

    $dX_t = \theta(\mu - X_t)dt + \sigma dW_t, \quad \text{Half-life} = \frac{\ln 2}{\theta}$
    • Stationary distribution is Gaussian with mean $\mu$ and asymptotic variance $\sigma^2 / (2\theta)$.
    • Half-life of mean reversion $t_{1/2} = \frac{\ln 2}{\theta}$ determines strategy holding period and rebalancing frequency.
    • Trading rule: Enter when the spread deviates by $> 2\sigma$ from $\mu$, exit when it crosses $\mu$.
    2.6

    Jump-Diffusion & Merton Model

    Merton's jump-diffusion model augments standard diffusion with a compound Poisson jump process to capture sudden market crashes, earnings surprises, and macro announcements: $dS_t = (\mu - \lambda k) S_t dt + \sigma S_t dW_t + (J-1) S_t dN_t$, where $N_t$ is a Poisson process with jump intensity $\lambda$ and $J$ is log-normal jump size.

    Mathematical Formulation

    $dS_t = (\mu - \lambda k)S_t dt + \sigma S_t dW_t + (J - 1)S_t dN_t$
    • Generates short-dated volatility smiles and negative skewness observed in index options.
    • Markets are incomplete when jumps exist: risks cannot be perfectly hedged with continuous Delta hedging alone.
    • Jump risk premium demands higher implied volatility for out-of-the-money put options.
